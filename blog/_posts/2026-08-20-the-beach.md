I once knew a GCU that had a pet beach that went on forever. Not a virtual
environment, I mean an honest-to-goodness physical beach sand, rocks, water,
plants, animals - the lot.

Now I know what you are thinking, those GCUs are *tiny*, barely a megatonne
most of them. We all know about the multiple massive environments that the big
GSVs maintain, often vast landscapes in a medium or main bay; or in a separate
but attached outrigger or a field bubble. I guess this little GCU just wanted
to get in on the act too.

To be fair, it didn't actually go on forever - it occupied the rear compartment
which had been given an organic looking extension looking from the outside a
bit like an insect's thorax. Inside a careful arrangement of rocks, screening
plants, fields and projection screens provided a pretty convincing impression
of a distant horizon and some tricks of forced perspective made it seem like
the landscape extended much farther to the sides - at least to those of us
viewing the spectrum of visible light.  As the GCU noted, most of the aquatic
life used echo or electrical field senses in addition to their eyes and knew
perfectly well that they were living in a big tank.

When questioned, the ship gave anwers of variable plausibility for why it chose
to haul this not inconsiderable mass across interstellar distances. Sometimes
it would suggest that this large body of water was a fire-fighting resource
(like it didn't have a hundred better ways to do that) and would offer the
questioner a species / civilizational appropriate example as a justification.
For it me it was Frank Lloyd-Wright putting Koi ponds in the lobby of the Grand
Imperial Hotel in Japan.

Sometimes it would claim that it was an environmental life support back up
system. In the event of a total system shutdown, say a nearby nuclear blast
causing an EMP (like even the humblest module wouldn't be bothered by *that*),
or say, having all its processing resources co-opted by a hegemonising swarm
then the oxygen-breating crew would be able to survive (or at least respirate)
for months based solely on the autonomous eco-system cycles so carefully put
into place.

Other times, and more believably perhaps, the ship would just say that it
simply enjoyed the challenge of designing a fully functional set of
interconnected organic systems that would meld into a stable state with minimal
ongoing interventions .A pet vivarium project really. The ship made its
challenge somewhat simpler by constructing the vivarium from a single, existing
planetary ecology. It had cajoled a nearby super-lifter to bring it a tank of
sea water and a selection of sand, rocks and a variety of  suspended animation
flora and fauna from the tropical zone of an earth-like planet in the Lesser
Forghast Cluster. The locals were about to bulldoze the area for a new harbour
and hotel complex so it couldn't even be accused of enivronmental
mis-appropriation.

The ship itself, according to some internal artfulness criteria of its own
individually placed every plant and rock, judiciously sculpting the landscape
like the work of art it was. It also created a semi-chaotic weather system
including rare but exciting low hurricane force winds.

It wasn't truly a closed system of course since it was open to the rest of the
ship, the rear crew lounge opened seamlessly into a wooden beach-side bar and
thence down to the sand so the atmosphere of the of the ship and its plaything
environment did mingle to some extent - not to mention the crew taking regular
swims in the "ocean" and shedding all sorts of skin cells and worse that were
not native to the original environment.

Moreover, the ship was justifiably proud of its debauched beach parties held
whenever it came across another Culture vessel or outpost; and despite needing
a small fleet of micro-drones to clean up afterward and recover the remarkable
about of detritus that was scattered about - drinking paraphenalia, food
scraps, paper streamers, discarded jewellery, carelessly tossed clothing and on
regular occasions complete but insensible party-goers drifting in the shallows
or draped across rocks.

Of course given the small physical size of the actual beach area party
attendance was strictly limited and by invitation of the ship only - something
that it used shamelessly to extract favours, promises and juicy gossip from
prospective and eager guests.

Most of the time however the beach literally looked after itself, a myriad of
creatures large and small locked in eternal combat or symbiotic cooperation
depending on their roles, unknowingly working together to maintain the
long-term stasis.

Despite that occaisional storm the original climate had been warm, with mild
breezes and much bright yellow sunshine so that's how I mainly experienced the
beach. I used to enjoy sitting at a bar stool simultaneously being back
somewhere safe and familiar and at the same time knowing that we were actually
in deep space with all that black unknown around us. Given the incredible
variation in colour and body pattern of terrestrial tropical fish, birds and
small mammals the creatures here didn't really seem all that "alien" to me and
I could watch humming bird analogues flitting serenely amongst the extravagant
flowers that lined the back of the beach and spilled into the bar area.

I'd never seen the birds in any other part of the vessel, nor indeed crash into
the sky projection. On enquiring the ship explained that it had enclosed the
vivarium in a mass-sensitive directional treacle field. Anything smaller than,
say, a cat, on encountering the field would continue to move its legs, wings or
other motile appendages as much as it wanted to without making any actual
progress so would soon get bored and turn around. Larger masses, such as the
crew were assumed to be sufficiently intelligent to be allowed the free run of
both ship proper and the beach environment and could decide on their own
cogniszance whether they wanted to throw themselves headlong into the
projection screens, and indeed frequently did so during the wilder parties.

For the party held in my honour, shortly after I had first joined the vessel
the ship had, very thoughtfully, got in touch with the original contact unit
and obtained a detailed catalogue of my home planet's various liquid
intoxicants, not just the chemical composition but the precise shape and
labelling of the bottles and typical delivery quantities and mechanisms.
Afterwards some of these bottles still adorned the shelvess at the back of the
bar and I think that the ship, in the form of its silver-skinned avatar quite
enjoyed the idea of making cocktails as it never ceased in trying to find
customers to try out its mixology skills upon.

I do sometimes wonder what happened to that GCU after I left it, and where it
is now. It has been more than a normal human lifetime since I moved on (yes, I
have taken the nano-medical interventions). In theory pretty much any
information that the Culture has is freely available to anyone that asks for
it. The current location of a System Class GSV is both hard to hide and their
itineries are published some years in advance anyway. For the smaller vessels,
those with a single controlling mind it can be harder to find out what is going
on. It is quite possible that I could send a message to the home GSV (if I
could remember its name, it will eventually come to me) and find out almost
instantly the current status. It is also possible that the GCU has changed its
affiliation, been lost to accident, gone rogue or the mind simply felt that it
had sufficiently paid back its creation debt to the culture (even though there
isn't really such an obligation), signed off the roster of active ships and
taken itself off into the dark black yonder. What's more, given the devious
and/or playful nature of ship minds it could make the records *appear* that any
of these things has actually happened and in reality be engaged in some
clandestine operation for  Special Circumstances. So yes, in theory I could ask
after it but I also knew I could never really trust any answer I got back.

So I don't ask, and I just like to think that somewhere out there in the galaxy
is a ship with a bulbous butt inside of which is a bar stocked with drink
bottles from old Earth on a beach that goes on forever.
