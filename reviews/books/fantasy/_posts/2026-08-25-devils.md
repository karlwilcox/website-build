There's nothing in this book that you haven't encountered many times before in the
fantasy genre. This is a good thing as we already know pretty much everything about
how this world works. What is really impressive is just what tremendous fun Joe
offers us from this familiar fare! There's almost non-stop action, fantastic
banter and every character is very much their own person, with their own voice
and their own bunch of problems, neruoses and heroic moments.

The story is built around a journey to take a exlied princess, raised as a street
orphan in the Holy City to reclaim her rightful throne in the mighty city of Troy.
Our putative Empress is escorted by a wonderful collection of misfits and criminals
from the Office of the Holy Expediency, (the "devils" of the title) lead by a
hapless librarian, Brother Diaz.

The action starts almost immediately and never really lets up anywhere in the
journey. I had a moment of doubt in Venice when our heroes set off on a side-quest
to gain passage across the Mediterranean. I was worried that we were going to
be treated to a series of "go get a thing to do a thing" exercises (a bit like the
first season of the Mandalorian) and would effectively get lost five layers deep
in nested missions. Fortunately the side quest was rapidly resolved and we immediately
boarded ship for Troy and the main thread continued at the usual fast pace. I saw
"fast", but this as a big book, almost 600 pages but it doesn't seem like it,
it is such a fun read and theres a lot going on so it doesn't seem over-long at all.

The final chapters are particularly poignant as the characters that we have really
developed a great rapport with, come to love and respect and find out their
ultimate fates, not all that we necessarily hoped for them. Most things are
neatly concluded and this is a good point to stop and rest before the
second volume comes out next year.

I'm giving this book a recommended tag because I think Joe does a fantastic
job of taking familiar fantasy building blocks and making something genuinely
much greater than the sum of its parts - an action-packed, fun journey
with great back chat and characters we really like, depsite their many, many
flaws! A real triumph.
