This really is a masterpiece of science fiction.
As well as being astonishingly prolific the author has
an astounding imagination as big as the planet of
Majipoor itself.

We open on a pastoral scene with our hero having
nothing of himself other than his name. He becomes
a travelling juggler (inspiring me to pick up my
own juggling balls again) and from that small
beginning attempts to regain the stolen throne, a
task that involves travelling across most of the vast
landmasses of the giant planet.

The world building is extraordinary, a low-metal
planet moving on almost medieval society but with
some elements of advanced technology left over
from the interstellar colonisation thousands of years
ago, and a range of non-human species largely
living in peaceful co-existence.

We visit fabulous cities, vast forests, deserts
and cross wide oceans encountering all sorts of
adventures and picking up retainers and troops on
the way. One of the strengths of the book is how
immersively real everything feels, helped enormously
by a confident use of background details and above
all names that roll off the tongue. Cities, plants,
animals, races and characters all have convincing
nomenclature, starting with Sleat, Carabella, Zalzan
Koaal and ending with the 50 cities of Castle
Mount, putting us right there in the midst of
this fantastic planet.

If he had written nothing else, Robert Silverberg would
still be a giant of Science Fiction.
