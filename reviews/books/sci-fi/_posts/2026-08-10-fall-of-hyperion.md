My matching paperbacks for the final three volumes of the Hyperion Cantos
arrived a couple of days before the offical release and are looking splendid on
my “Space Fiction Hall of Fame” shelf.

Before passing on the previous ill-matched set I decided to re-read “The Fall”
and crease the spine of that one instead.

This book is another triumph of imagination and plotting. Instead of the first
book’s fairly simple frame narrative of the pilgrims’ journey and each telling
their tales in flashback, “The Fall” is a much more complex book. As well as
the ongoing adventures of the pilgrims we follow a host of other characters,
primarily revolving around Meina Gladstone, Hegemony CEO and Joseph Severn, a
cybrid restored personality of John Keats.

The story has a lot of moving parts, both in terms of location (genuinely
galaxy spanning) and timing (there’s an intricate chain of events across
multiple time lines). Dan Simmons’ skill is revealed by the way all this
complexity unfolds so well, with not a wasted page. There is a lot of wonder
and spectacle and a (necessarily quite long) conclusion where all the characters
are (more or less) neatly wrapped up, yet enough remains unresolved for a
further two books of equal size!

I’m going to leave the Endymion to one side for now as the stories are fairly
fresh in my mind from an audiobook session during lockdown, but don’t think
that I wasn’t tempted to pick them up and lose myself in the far future again!
